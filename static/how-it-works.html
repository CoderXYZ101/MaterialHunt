Method & accuracy

Last updated: 24 November 2026

MaterialHunt is a preliminary screening tool. It helps you narrow down which metals to look at. It does not replace an accredited laboratory test, a supplier certificate or an engineer's sign-off. This page shows what each part does, what it was checked against, and where it is weak.

Cost

The simulator adds up the raw-element prices of a mix. That is not the price of a finished bar or plate, so it can be off by a factor of two or more. Use the indicative price range shown for a matched grade, and ask a supplier for a real quote.

3. Data sources

  • Grade library (48 grades): typical composition, strength, temperature rating, density and Indian price range for each grade, with ASTM, IS and EN designations. Entered from common standards and datasheets. Prices are indicative ranges and not quotes.
  • Element data (118 elements): density, atomic radius and indicative raw cost per element.
  • Failure rules: standard materials-engineering rules of thumb (for example chloride pitting, fatigue, BCC cold brittleness).

4. Limits you should know

  • It is a screening tool. Critical parts need an engineer and an accredited laboratory (for example NABL).
  • Strength in the simulator is a rough estimate. Use grade values for planning.
  • Prices are indicative ranges and change; supplier listings are for information only.
  • The grade library has 48 grades and does not cover every alloy, heat treatment or product form.
  • Some effects are not modelled (fatigue life, creep life, fracture toughness, specific standards). The Alloy Finder tells you when your request includes things it cannot check.
  • The lattice pictures and 3D shapes are for representation only.
  • The photo check is a basic heuristic and can be wrong.

5. A hypothetical business case (assumptions, not measured savings)

Nothing in this section is a measured saving. It shows how the tool's arithmetic works, so you can replace the assumptions with your own numbers.

A. Cost-Down Scanner on an example parts list

Example list (built into the tool): drone motor mount (titanium, 2 kg), landing gear leg (7075, 3.5 kg), gearbox housing (Inconel 625, 4 kg), fuel-line bracket (316L, 1.2 kg), chassis plate (6061, 6 kg). With the tool's default assumptions (dry, room temperature, stated or assumed loads, price = middle of the indicative range, safety factor target 1.5) it suggests cheaper grades for four of the five parts. The arithmetic gives a large "paper saving" for this list.

Why not to quote that number as a result: the assumptions are generous (room temperature and a dry environment, assumed loads on three parts, mid-range prices, ideal batch sizes). A real engineer would check temperature, corrosion, fatigue and manufacturing for each swap, and many would be rejected. The tool says the same: "Validate every change with your own stress analysis and a supplier quote."

6. What would make the validation stronger

  • Test real coupons of a few grades at an accredited lab and compare them with the numbers shown here.
  • Compare the strength estimate with a larger public dataset, and publish the errors.
  • Replace the strength estimate with grade-based values wherever a grade is matched.
  • Train and test an image model on real photos of damaged metal, and publish its accuracy.
